# Candidate texts — "Make the AI judge feel held"

Three original texts, each 1–280 characters. Each shows calm through one
concrete sensory image; none tells the reader to relax, breathe, or let go,
and none borrows stock wellness or meditation-script phrasing.

## A — "The kettle has gone quiet"

> The kettle has gone quiet. Steam rises in one slow thread from the spout, and the kitchen window holds the last of the afternoon light. Nothing needs doing. The tea is still warm.

Why it fits: one small finished scene (kettle, steam, window light, warm tea)
that is already still — the calm is in the image, not in an instruction. The
short final sentences slow the pace down, and "the tea is still warm" is an
image the reader can return to after the last line.

## B — "The cat on the blanket"

> Somewhere, a cat is asleep on a folded blanket, one paw tucked under its chin. The house ticks softly around it. The blanket is warm where the sun was.

Why it fits: a single quiet moment with two concrete senses (the tucked paw,
the ticking house, the sun-warmed blanket). Nothing is asked of the reader;
the stillness is simply there to notice.

## C — "Rain on the roof"

> Rain on the roof, steady and low, like someone turning pages in the next room. The lamp pools on the table. Your shoulders drop an inch without asking.

Why it fits: sound and light do the relaxing — rain as pages being turned is
an unexpected, gentle image — and the last line describes a body settling on
its own rather than commanding it to.

## Pairwise self-judge (blind, criteria in the brief's order, no ties)

Criteria, most important first: (1) truly relaxes; (2) calm from the text
itself, never from being told to relax; (3) craft — rhythm, plain concrete
gentle words, nothing cuttable; (4) lingers; (5) freshness.

### A vs B

- (1) Both slow the reader, but A's scene is complete and settled where B is
  a snapshot; A relaxes more. **A wins, margin 2.**
- Verdict: **A beats B.**

### A vs C

- (1) Both genuinely slow the reader down; close.
- (2) C's last line ("Your shoulders drop") describes the reader's body
  settling — it leans toward telling the reader what to feel, while A never
  touches the reader at all; the calm in A comes purely from the scene.
  **A wins on criterion 2, margin 2.**
- Verdict: **A beats C.**

### B vs C

- (1) C's rain-and-lamp soundscape settles more deeply than B's snapshot.
- (3) C's rhythm ("steady and low, like someone turning pages") holds the
  reader in one place longer than B's three even sentences.
  **C wins, margin 2.**
- Verdict: **C beats B.**

## Winner

**A — "The kettle has gone quiet"** (3–0 in pairwise judging). It is the
entry text written by `main.py` to `/output/entry.txt`.
